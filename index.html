👋 What Is Kinesis?

Kinesis is a Windows application that lets you operate your computer without touching a mouse or keyboard. It watches you through a single webcam, recognises your hand movements, and turns them into real mouse actions on your screen.

Where your eyes look decides which monitor or window is in focus. What your hands do decides what happens there. You can click, scroll, drag, minimise a window, switch between open apps with Alt-Tab, move between browser tabs, and even type by speaking out loud — all without lifting your hands to a device.

It is built for people who want a calmer desk: accessibility users who cannot use a traditional mouse comfortably, presenters who want to control slides from across a room, streamers who need both hands free, and anyone with a multi-monitor setup who is tired of dragging a cursor across three screens.

Everything runs locally on your own machine. There is no account to create, no subscription, and no cloud service to sign in to.

🎯 What You Can Do With Kinesis

  • Point with your hand — move the cursor by moving your hand in the air, with smoothing that removes the jitter you normally get from webcam tracking.
  • Click and double-click — a simple finger gesture presses the left mouse button for you.
  • Scroll — move your hand up or down to scroll long pages, documents, and chat threads.
  • Drag and drop — hold a window, a file, or a slider and move it where you want it.
  • Minimise and restore — clear a window out of the way with one gesture and bring it back just as easily.
  • Alt-Tab between apps — jump between running programs without reaching for the keyboard.
  • Switch browser tabs — flick between tabs in your browser the same way.
  • Dictate hands-free — speak, and Kinesis types it into whatever text box is active, using Handy dictation.
  • Stream while you work — the built-in virtual camera keeps your real webcam available to other apps such as OBS Studio.

🖱️ Your First Five Minutes

When you launch Kinesis for the first time, it walks you through a short calibration. This is how the app learns where your screen is in relation to your camera, and how your eyes and hand movements map to it.

  1. Choose your camera. If you have more than one webcam plugged in, pick the one you want Kinesis to use. A preview window confirms the camera is working.
  2. Calibrate your gaze. A series of dots appears across each of your monitors in turn. Look at each dot until it confirms. This step is what makes "eyes pick the window" feel natural.
  3. Set your hand range. Hold your hand up in front of the camera and move it around the area you are comfortable using. The app records the comfortable zone so you never have to stretch.
  4. Try a click. Perform the click gesture as shown on screen and watch the cursor respond. If it is too sensitive or not sensitive enough, adjust the slider in settings.
  5. Turn on dictation if you want it. Enable Handy in the dictation tab and pick your microphone. Speak normally — the text appears where your cursor is.

If anything feels wrong during the first run, re-run calibration. It takes under a minute and fixes the majority of "the cursor goes to the wrong place" problems.

Kinesis uses gaze tracking to work out which screen and which window you are looking at. When you glance at your second monitor, the controls follow you there. You do not need to drag a cursor across the desk or shuffle windows around.

This is especially useful on wide desks. Look at your notes on the left screen, look at the video call on the right, and your hand keeps working wherever your attention is. Within a single window, your gaze also tells Kinesis where to pay attention, which makes clicks land far more accurately than hand tracking alone.

    🎥 The Virtual Camera

    Kinesis includes a virtual camera — a pretend webcam that other apps can use as if it were a real piece of hardware. This matters if you stream, present, or join video calls.

    Without a virtual camera, Kinesis would need exclusive access to your physical webcam, so OBS Studio, Zoom, Teams, or Discord would see a black screen or a "camera in use" error. With the virtual camera, you can keep Kinesis running and still send video to your streaming or meeting app.

    You do not have to configure anything special. The virtual camera is installed alongside Kinesis and appears in the camera list of your other applications. Just select it there instead of your physical webcam.

    ⚡ Performance and Smoothness

    Kinesis is designed to feel responsive. It processes roughly 46 milliseconds of latency from the moment you move to the moment the cursor responds — fast enough that your brain treats it as direct control rather than a delayed signal.

    The cursor is smoothed with a filter that removes the natural tremor of a webcam feed while still following quick movements. Small adjustments, like placing a cursor between two letters, remain possible. Large sweeps, like throwing the cursor across a monitor, stay fast.

    Because all the tracking happens locally, your network speed has no effect on responsiveness. A slow internet connection does not make the cursor lag.

    🔧 Tips for Reliable Tracking

    • Light your face, not your back. A lamp or window in front of you helps the camera see your hand clearly. Backlighting makes every gesture less accurate.
    • Keep your hand inside the frame. If the cursor freezes, check whether your hand has drifted out of the camera's view.
    • Calibrate per monitor. If you add a new screen, run the gaze calibration again so Kinesis knows where the new display sits.
    • Re-calibrate after moving your webcam. Even a few centimetres of movement can shift the mapping between your hand and the cursor.
    • Use a stand. A webcam on a flexible stand is easier to angle consistently than one perched on a monitor.

    ❓ Common Questions

    Does Kinesis send my camera feed anywhere?

    No. All tracking and dictation processing happens on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded to a server, and no account is required.

    Can I use it alongside my normal mouse and keyboard?

    Yes. Kinesis does not lock you out of your other input devices. You can move your mouse at any time, and Kinesis will pick up from wherever you left off.

    Will it work with my laptop's built-in camera?

    Yes. Any webcam that Windows can see will work. Built-in laptop cameras are usually fine, though a slightly higher-resolution external camera can make tracking smoother.

    What happens if I only have one monitor?

    Kinesis works perfectly with a single screen. The eye-tracking still helps it understand which window you are paying attention to, and the hand control still moves the cursor.

    Is there a cost?

    No. Kinesis is free to download and use.
